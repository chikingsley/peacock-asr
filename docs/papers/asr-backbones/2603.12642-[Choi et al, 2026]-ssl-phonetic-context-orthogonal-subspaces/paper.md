Investigates how self-supervised speech models encode phonetic context within their internal representations, finding that different phonetic contexts are encoded in position-dependent orthogonal subspaces across layers. The analysis reveals a structured geometry in SSL representations where phonetic information is systematically organized, providing theoretical grounding for why layer selection and aggregation strategies matter. Highly relevant to pronunciation assessment research as it explains what information is available at different layers and how phonetic context influences the representation space that GOP and similar scoring methods operate over.
